Q2
Bill, Judy, Sally have the occupation of the teacher, plumber and teamster, But not necessary in that order. Bill is shorter than Judy but taller than Sally. The plumber is the tallest and teamster is the shortest, what is the Judy's occupation?
Algorithm:
Step 1: Read the given information:
Occupations: Teacher, plumber, teamster.
Bill is shorter than Judy but taller than Sally.
The plumber is the tallest.
The teamster is the shortest.
Step 2: Deduce the height order: Judy > Bill > Sally.
Step 3: Assign the tallest occupation (plumber) to the tallest person (Judy).
Step 4: Assign the shortest occupation (teamster) to the shortest person (Sally).
Step 5: Assign the remaining occupation (teacher) to the remaining person (Bill).
Step 6: Output Judy's occupation (plumber).

1.  Explain the Problem-Solving Life Cycle Steps, apply it to the following problem. Write down the algorithm (general stepwise execution) & draw the flowchart.
	a. There is a goat, a tiger, a man and a bundle of grass on one side of a 		river. There's a boat which can only carry two at a time and can only be 	rowed manually by the man. How can they cross the river
	b. There are FOUR temples, one plant of flower and a pond. A boy goes 	there and before entering the first temple he plucks the flower and has to 	take a bath in the pond before entering into every temple. The specialty of 	the pond is that if a person is carrying flowers with him and he takes a dip 	in the pond the number of the flowers gets doubled. He takes a dip and 	offers some flowers in the first temple, then he again takes a dip and offers 	flowers in second temple, and again the same in the third temple. The boy 	offers EQUAL number of flowers in all the 3 temples and goes back with 	EMPTY hand after offering flower in the third temple. How many flowers 	could he have offered in each temple?
Solution:

(a) Flowchart for River Crossing Problem
1.	Start
2.	Man takes the goat across the river and leaves it there.
3.	Man returns alone to the starting side.
4.	Man takes the tiger across the river and leaves it there.
5.	Man brings the goat back to the starting side.
6.	Man takes the grass across the river and leaves it with the tiger.
7.	Man returns alone to the starting side.
8.	Man takes the goat across the river again.
9.	End

(b) Temple & Flower Problem
Problem Identification
• The boy plucks 1 flower before entering the first temple.
• He must bathe in the pond before entering each temple, and each bath
doubles the number of flowers he has.
• He offers equal flowers in each of the 3 temples.
• He ends up with zero flowers after the third temple.
Algorithm (Stepwise Execution)
1. Boy plucks 1 flower before entering the first temple.
2. Takes a bath in the pond —> flowers double to 2.
3. Offers X flowers in the first temple.
4. Takes a bath in the pond —> remaining flowers double.
5. Offers X flowers in the second temple.
6. Takes a bath in the pond —> remaining flowers double.
7. Offers X flowers in the third temple.
8.Ends up with zero flowers.

Solving for X:
Let the flowers he offers in each temple be X.
After plucking, he has: 1 flower.

First bath: Flowers double —> 2.
After first temple: (2 - X) remain.
Second bath: Remaining flowers double —>(2 - X) * 2
After second temple: (4 - 2X - X) = 4 - 3X remain.
Third bath: Flowers double —> (4 - 3X) * 2 = 8 - 6X.
After third temple: (8 - 6X - X) = 8 - 7X = 0.
Solving for X:
8-7x=0
7X=8
x = 8/7
Since X must be a whole number, the problem needs 1 initial flower to be
replaced with a higher starting number (possibly 2 or 4) to make it work.
